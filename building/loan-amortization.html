Loan Amortization Schedule Tool

December 3, 2024

Preview or download this tool!

Taking out a loan is one thing, but understanding how that loan will get paid off over time is another. There are a lot of questions you may have when taking out a loan.

How much of each payment goes toward interest? How much actually reduces the balance? How much interest will I pay over the entire life of the loan?

These are important questions, which this Loan Amortization Schedule tool was designed to help answer.


How the Loan Amortization Calculator Works

The tool is built in Excel and does the calculations for you. All you need to do is enter the loan information in the corresponding section, including the loan amount, annual interest rate, loan term, first payment date, payment frequency, compounding period, and payment timing. Once you enter your information, the calculator automatically generates your payment schedule.

The schedule breaks down each payment so you can see exactly how your loan balance changes over time. It includes the payment number, due date, payment amount, interest paid, principal paid, and remaining balance after each payment. You can also enter additional payments to see how paying extra might affect your loan balance.


Beginning vs. End of Period Payment Types

In loan calculations and time-value-of-money (TVM) analysis, begin mode means payments are made at the start of each period, while end mode means payments are made at the end of each period. Standard consumer and mortgage loans always use end mode!

Making payments at the beginning of a period reduces the outstanding balance slightly sooner, which lowers the total interest paid over the life of the loan. However, standard loan contracts may not require or allow begin-mode payments unless structured as a specialized lease or advance agreement.


Seeing the Bigger Picture

Beyond showing each individual payment, the calculator provides a summary of your loan, including the periodic interest rate, total number of payments, and calculated payment amount. Most importantly, it helps you understand the total interest paid and total principal repaid over the life of the loan.

For example, a $100,000 loan with a 6% annual interest rate over 30 years can have a very different long-term cost than you might initially expect. Looking at the full amortization schedule helps you see how interest accumulates and how your payments gradually reduce the principal balance.

Whether you're evaluating a mortgage, a personal loan, or another type of installment loan, this calculator provides a practical way to explore the numbers and make more informed financial decisions.
