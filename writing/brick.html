Brick

April 3, 2023
"Maybe I'll just sit here and bleed at you."

Brick is a neo-noir mystery thriller written and directed by Rian Johnson.

Synopsis: "A teenage loner pushes his way into the underworld of a high school crime ring to investigate the disappearance of his ex-girlfriend."

The first time I tried to watch this, about 15 minutes went by before I was completely lost. The story is told through a series of twists and turns that can be confusing at times. Those who aren't familiar with film noir tropes may have difficulty keeping up at first.

This film is a prime example of creativity maximized through limitations. With a budget of 450k USD, it grossed 3.9M USD. With the feel of a student film fueling its tone and aesthetic, this film is self-aware, parodying a genre while embracing it and its tropes. It's a unique film that blends elements of a classic film noir with a modern high school drama. These disparate genres are seamlessly merged into a cohesive and engaging story.

The stylized and moody visuals add to the overall atmosphere. The framing is intriguing and intentional. Notably, many of the characters are identifiable solely (no pun intended) by their shoes.

Overall, this movie was surprisingly beautiful and entertaining. The unique blend of genres and strong performances make it a standout film. Also, it was kind of fun to see Joseph Gordon-Levitt get knocked out a bunch.

★★★★★
